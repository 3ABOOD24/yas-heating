How to choose the right heating system for your home

Start with the building, not the brand. Wall area, glazing, insulation and how many hours a day a room is really used all change the right answer. As a rule of thumb: radiators suit renovations where floors can't be opened up, underfloor heating suits new builds and villas with the floor build-up already planned, and combi boilers make sense for apartments where space is tight. We size every system to a room-by-room heat-loss calculation rather than a flat per-square-metre estimate, which is usually where oversized, inefficient installs come from.

Underfloor heating: comfort, efficiency, and design freedom

Underfloor systems heat a room from a large, low-temperature surface instead of a few hot radiators, so the warmth is even from floor to ceiling and there's no wall space lost to hardware. They typically run at lower flow temperatures than radiators, which pairs well with solar-assisted or heat-pump setups and can reduce running costs over a heating season. The trade-off is response time — underfloor systems heat and cool more slowly, so they suit spaces that are occupied for long, predictable stretches rather than rooms that need to warm up in minutes.

Solar water heating: what to consider

A well-sized solar thermal system can cover a large share of a household's hot-water demand for most of the year in New Cairo's climate, with a conventional boiler or electric element as backup for cloudy stretches and winter. The two things that decide whether it's worth it are roof orientation — south-facing with minimal shading performs best — and tank sizing relative to how many people actually use hot water at once. We survey the roof and usage pattern before quoting, since an undersized tank is the most common reason people are disappointed with solar.
